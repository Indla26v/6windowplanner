04 / GOOD TO KNOW

A few things,
cleared up.

What does “six windows” mean?+

One subscription can overlap six weekly quota periods. Start before a reset, span four complete weeks, and finish after the final reset. The first and last windows are short sprints, so this does not mean six full weeks of paid access.

THE PLANNER’S 30-DAY EXAMPLE
  1. 01 · Entry sprint24 hours

    Thu 6:30 am → Fri 6:30 am. Use the quota still available before the next reset.

  2. 02–05 · Four full weeks4 × 168 hours

    Four Friday-to-Friday periods, each starting at the scheduled 6:30 am reset.

  3. 06 · Exit sprint24 hours

    Final Fri 6:30 am → Sat 6:30 am, between the last reset and subscription expiry.

24h + (4 × 168h) + 24h = 720h = 30 days

This example uses a Friday 6:30 am IST reset, with 24-hour entry and exit sprints. Start on Thursday morning and finish on Saturday morning 30 days later. Actual sprint lengths vary with the reset, start time, and billing period.

Buying again does not create a reset. In the strategy’s model, a week that is already 40% used has only 60% left until its scheduled reset. Re-entering that same week cannot be counted as another fresh quota.

How do 60 planned windows compare with 54 per year?+

For ten six-window purchases, the segment count is 10 × 6 = 60: 10 entry sprints, 40 full-week segments, and 10 exit sprints. That is six more planned segments than the standard 54-window comparison shown in the extension.

The counts measure different things. The screenshot also reports 53 of 54 distinct weekly caps usable, with seven entry sprints sharing an already-used week. Short segments and shared weeks are not additional independent quotas, and consuming a full cap during a short sprint is not guaranteed.

The strategy document uses 52 full weeks as its simplified annual baseline; this page uses the extension’s dated 54-window baseline. The exact count depends on the date range and partial weeks at its boundaries.

Does this give me more Claude quota?+

No. The planner helps you understand and schedule the windows available under your subscription. Claude’s plan limits still apply.

How does account detection work?+

The extension can read the weekly reset and usage percentage from the Claude account you’re signed in to in that browser. As described in the extension, that detected data stays in the extension. You can also enter your setup manually.

Can I see the trade-offs before subscribing?+

Yes. The yearly view compares planned purchases, usable weekly windows, cost per window, and paid days. The calendar distinguishes weekend rest days from weekdays spent on the free tier.
